How to obtain a certificate of completion

After finishing a lunch that required all sorts of care, I feel relieved. From the afternoon, it's history class.

I go back to the classroom and skim through the second-year history textbook. Like in Japanese, if it's Patience, it's a breeze. It seems like even I could pass if I studied a little.

But you can only skip a grade once a year. Besides, skipping a grade is only permission to take classes in a higher grade. Unless I obtain a certificate of completion like I did for magical practice, I won't be able to find the time to improve the lives of the Granger family.

It’s painful that being an attendant to Princess Margaret takes up more time than I thought. I really wish Louise would enter the dormitory and take over as an attendant.

There were no first-year students who skipped a grade in history. I feel somewhat relieved that Prince Keith and Louise are not there.

I take the class seriously and head to the library when it's over. I'm happy that I can pass magical practice. I want to use this time to research the scope of study for the third year of middle school.

"There's no one here. Oh, I see, it's during class."

It's convenient that it's empty. It would be troublesome if someone thought it was cheeky for me to be researching the middle school curriculum for mathematics, in particular.

"The textbooks aren't in the library, are they? If there were reference books or something, I could understand the general content, but..."

I look for books that might be helpful for learning by reading the spines, but I don't find them so easily. In times like these, I should ask the librarian.

"Excuse me. Do you have any reference books for third-year middle school mathematics?"

The librarian looks at me with a puzzled expression across the desk. Patience is small even for a first-year student due to a lack of nutrition. They probably thought it was strange for such a child to be looking for middle school reference books. But they taught me properly.

"If you're looking for third-year middle school mathematics reference books, they are kept around here."

There were reference books placed all along that shelf. It's been since high school that I've done math. In other words, I've forgotten quite a bit. I pick one up and flip through it. Yeah, it's about Math I. It'll probably get harder when it gets to the university level. It seems I could get a certificate of completion if I review a little, but how do I get that? I'll go ask Mr. Kepner.

Ideally, I should study before asking, but I don't want to waste any effort. I was quite shocked by the disappointment of skipping a grade.

I'll also ask if I can skip to middle school. After borrowing one reference book, I head to the staff room.

『Why is the staff room so hard to enter?』

I've been bad at entering staff rooms since my previous life. Even though I hadn't done anything wrong.

I call out, "Excuse me," and enter the staff room. Mr. Kepner was grading homework, but he notices me and beckons me over.

"Patience, oh, you received a certificate of completion for magical practice, didn't you? So, do you have any questions?"

As expected, Mr. Kepner is easy to talk to.

"Um, how can I obtain a certificate of completion?"

He widens his eyes in a bit of surprise. If it were Nashius, it would be cute, but I don't have a taste for middle-aged men.

"Patience, you are excellent, after all. Which subject would you like to take? But it will be quite difficult because it covers up to the third year of middle school."

"I would like to obtain a certificate of completion for mathematics, but I don't know how to go about it."

Mr. Kepner struck the desk with his hand, as if he had suddenly thought of something. What? Don't startle me like that.

"I see, you're the daughter of Viscount William Granger. In that case, basic math must be boring for you."

Huh, what? Was my father such a famous scholar? I only know him as someone who, after being dismissed from his post, just reads newspapers and books in his study.

"For the certificate of completion, you just need to achieve excellent results on the final exam. Since you have to take the final exam for the third year of middle school, it would stand out if a primary school student like you took it. I will discuss it with the math teacher."

"Thank you very much. I will study for a year."

A year, huh... I stifle a sigh, realizing that until then, only the practical magic classes will be exempted.

"No, the earliest would be the spring semester final exam."

"Eh? But wouldn't we not have finished studying the middle school curriculum by the spring semester?"

I wonder if it’s okay to issue a certificate of completion when the exam range only covers up to the spring semester.

"Well, I’ve made it so that the final major problem cannot be solved unless one understands the scope of middle school. That applies to other subjects as well."

I see!

"Besides, it's better to study other things than to attend classes you've already mastered."

Ugh, I have a bad feeling about this. The trauma of skipping grades being a waste of time comes flooding back.

"Um, if I get the completion certificate, I won't have to attend classes, right?" This is important.

"Ah, the primary school schedule is fixed, you see. If you were in middle school, you could take classes you're interested in, though."

Why does he sound so disappointed? Let me ask a question based on that middle school flow.

"Next year, when I become a third-year student, will I be able to skip a grade to middle school?"

"In theory, it's possible. It would be a waste of time not to let a student who has mastered the primary school content skip to middle school. But it's difficult. Also, if you get completion certificates for all the required subjects, you should be able to skip to middle school. Well, there hasn't been such a student until now, though."

I see, if I get completion certificates for all required subjects, I can move to middle school starting from the beginning of next year. It's a bit of a reliance on Patience for now, but I'll do my best!

"That's right, I'll give you the third-year primary school textbooks. And the middle school math textbook as well."

Mr. Kepner, you're such a good person! But, this is incredibly heavy.

I have Mr. Kepner's classical literature textbook on hand, but he asked a teacher he knows for the others.

That's right, the math one was from Mr. Hagan, my previous homeroom teacher.

I was encouraged, being told, "Are you going to challenge the completion certificate? Good luck!"

"Mr. Kepner, Mr. Hagan, thank you very much."

The two of them smiled faintly.

"Well, the field of education owes a debt of gratitude to Viscount Granger. Besides, it is a teacher's duty to support students who study hard."

"Patience, if there is anything you don't understand in math, come and ask."

What on earth did my father do? I'd like to look it up.
