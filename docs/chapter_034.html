To the Royal Palace to meet the Queen

On Friday, just as Mr. Kepner and Princess Margaret expected, I also received a certificate of completion for music. Mozart is a genius, after all. I felt like I had cheated when the teacher praised me so highly.

The art teacher told me that if I learned a little more about how to use paints, they would be willing to give me a certificate of completion as well. The Granger family didn’t have the money to buy paints, so Patience wasn’t used to them, either. Besides, I wasn’t used to the paints of this other world. They resemble oil paints, but it’s like grinding down stones or using mineral pigments. Perhaps they are closer to old-fashioned oil paints.

Yes, the reason I am indulging in this escapism is that I am currently in a carriage heading to the Royal Palace with Princess Margaret and her maid, Sophie. The Royal Palace is right next door. We arrived quickly. And yet, the carriage still wouldn’t stop. I wonder if members of the dismissed Granger family aren’t allowed to enter from the front?

"Patience, you’re making a strange face. The front of the Royal Palace is where administrative work is conducted. We live in the detached palace in the back."

After going halfway around the Royal Palace, the detached palace came into view. It is a slightly smaller but more luxurious building than the Royal Palace. My heart is pounding. Trusting in Patience’s etiquette, I follow Princess Margaret.

I want to see all sorts of things inside the detached palace, but looking around restlessly seems to be a breach of etiquette. I head toward the Queen’s chambers.

"Mother, I’m back. This is my lady-in-waiting, Patience Granger."

I pinch the skirt of my uniform and perform a deep bow. Patience’s voice is buzzing in my head, reminding me that I must not lift my head until I am spoken to, and it’s giving me a headache.

"Patience, please lift your head."

I was finally released from the bow. I looked up to see Queen Victoria. "Noble beauty" must be a phrase coined for the Queen. She possesses a youthfulness and beauty that makes it hard to believe she has given birth to five children.

"I am Patience Granger."

When I greeted her, I was offered a chair. It’s okay to sit, right? Princess Margaret nodded, so I sat down with proper decorum.

"Patience, your mother Julianne and I were friends at the Royal Academy."

Since nobles are required to attend the Royal Academy, that is not surprising.

"Mother, is that why you chose Patience to be my lady-in-waiting?"

To Princess Margaret’s question, Queen Victoria smiled and asked in return.

"Margaret, how do you find Patience as your lady-in-waiting?"

Technically, answering a question with a question is a breach of etiquette. But the Queen and Princess Margaret are mother and daughter, and it is not my place to comment.

"Patience is a good lady-in-waiting. Even setting aside the matter of her mother."

Satisfied with that, the Queen nodded. At that signal, tea and cake were placed before us.

After the Queen and Princess Margaret took a sip, I also drank my tea. As expected, the high-quality tea leaves have a lovely aroma. The cake is... I see that even in the Royal Palace, the sugar is gritty.

Higher-grade cafeteria

The difference from there is that it is cut into bite-sized pieces. I can eat this much.

As a lady-in-waiting, I must not interject in the conversation between mother and daughter, so I listen in silence.

"Are you waking up properly in the morning? You must eat breakfast."

"Yes, I am eating it properly."

That’s right; I wake her up and even help her get dressed so she can have breakfast.

"Well, that is wonderful progress. Lord Alfred will surely be pleased as well."

In other words, Princess Margaret had been oversleeping even at the detached palace. The lady-in-waiting must have had a hard time.

"Studies are important. You must learn diligently."

"Yes, I am learning diligently."

I help a little with her math homework, but she solved all the problems herself.

"More than that, Mother, Patience has a talent for music. Come now, play Hanon."

Princess Margaret seems to want to avoid talking about her school life with the Queen any further. She ordered a performance of Hanon as a way to change the subject.

I play a Mozart sonata.

"Marvelous. Julianne was also good at Hanon, but yours is exceptional."

Being highly praised makes me feel a bit guilty, but since I haven't received any cheat-like abilities, I'll let this slide. Ah, maybe that strange daily-life magic is a cheat, though.

After that, I played a few new songs for the music club, and it looks like I’ll manage to finish greeting the Queen without incident.

"Patience, I must give you a reward for that wonderful Hanon performance."

I was asked if there was anything I wanted. Patience is pestering me not to say I want money.

I follow Patience's lead and decline at first, as it is manners to do so, but the Queen will not take "no" for an answer. However, Patience is checking everything to make sure it isn't something expensive.

"Then, I would be happy if I could have some eggs, butter, sugar, and heavy cream."

Queen Victoria and Princess Margaret make strange faces.

"That much is fine, but I wonder why you would want those?"

"I want to bake treats for my younger brothers."

The Queen had guessed that the Granger family was struggling financially after Viscount Granger was dismissed, but she hadn't thought it was to the extent that a young lady would stand in the kitchen herself.

"To think..."

"No, I used to bake snacks with my late mother several times. My brothers probably don't remember, so I want to make them for them to eat."

In reality, we are even poorer than the Queen thinks, but that is not something that should be said. The Granger family is proud.

"My, that is a very lovely memory. Indeed, your brothers are young and likely do not clearly remember their mother. You must teach them the taste of their mother well."

It is generally not considered very praiseworthy for a young lady to stand in the kitchen, but baking sweets with a mother seems to be acceptable. With this, I can eat my favorite sweets with my brothers.

The meeting with the Queen finally ended, and I was taken back to the mansion in a magnificent royal carriage. The accompanying maid is holding a basket containing the eggs, sugar, butter, and heavy cream. Plus, I get to see my brothers a day early. I'm so happy!
