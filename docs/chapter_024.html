Is everything in hell also decided by money? Or is money alone not enough?

I enter the study, feeling as though my father

has ruined the time

I was going to spend with my younger brothers.

"Patience, you did well to skip a grade. I am proud of you."

I am happy to be praised, but my younger brothers are waiting.

"Thank you. I will continue to do my best."

I quickly rose from the chair, exited the study, and tried to hurry to the nursery.

"Wait a moment. We aren't finished yet."

I noticed two envelopes on the magnificent desk in my father's study. One had the same seal as the Royal Academy's emblem, so it was likely the notification regarding the grade skip. But the other one... that crest! Did Prince Keith lodge a complaint about me? Perhaps that it was presumptuous of me to pass more subjects than he did? I turned pale.

"Patience, are you alright?"

I might have brought a new calamity upon the unfortunate Granger family. Yet my father is buoyant.

"Yes, I apologize for being flustered. What is that letter?"

"Ah, this is a letter from Queen Victoria."

As I suspected, Prince Keith must have told on me to the Queen. I wanted to faint, but I have never fainted before.

"She says she wants you to serve as a lady-in-waiting to Princess Margaret. What an honorable proposal. Apparently, Princess Margaret lives in the dormitory. King Alfred certainly provides a strict education, given that Princes Richard and Keith also live in the dorms."

My father speaks in such a respectful tone toward King Alfred, even though he was supposedly fired by him. More importantly, what is a "lady-in-waiting"? It's different from a maid, right?

"Father, what is a lady-in-waiting?"

Patience doesn't seem to know either, so I ask my father, who used to work at the royal palace. I'm a bit worried since he was fired, though.

"A lady-in-waiting is someone who serves personally at the side of a queen or princess."

It's no wonder he got fired. My father's explanation makes absolutely no sense.

"What is the difference between a lady-in-waiting and a maid?"

I demand a proper explanation from my father, who is giddy about the honor. If I don't ask for specifics, I'll be the one in trouble later.

"A lady-in-waiting is a public servant who works at the royal palace. A maid is a servant. A lady-in-waiting is a noble who serves royalty personally. To think you've been chosen as Princess Margaret's lady-in-waiting!"

Unlike my father, who is elated by the honor, I feel nothing but confusion.

"Princess Margaret is a third-year student, isn't she? I skipped a grade, but I am still only a second-year student..."

I don't want to be like Ralph or Hugo, who are glued to Prince Keith. Besides, if we are in different grades, our classes will be different, so wouldn't serving her be impossible? I put my hope into that, wishing for him to implicitly decline. I feel like Patience is excited too, but I'm ignoring that!

"No, apparently that is fine, as she has study companions during class hours. While Prince Richard and Prince Keith's study companions moved into the dorms with them, Princess Margaret's companions are young ladies, and it seems they refrained from moving into the dorms starting in their third year."

I would like to refrain as well. But my father shows no signs of wanting to decline.

"Would you mind if I took some time to think about it?"

Father seems to have been shocked. Is it impossible to refuse such an honorable offer? It doesn't seem like a good idea to ask him, but I should ask Wyatt the butler if there's any pay involved. Money talks, after all.

I filled up my energy to face Wyatt by helping my younger brothers with their studies for a while, and by taking them to the greenhouse to pull up turnips together.

Mary is busy taking care of my younger brothers until it's time to change for dinner. I’ll use that gap to head to the butler's office in the semi-basement. If Mary finds out about the offer to be Princess Margaret's lady-in-waiting, she definitely won't let me refuse. It means I can't stand the thought of disappointing Mary.

"Wyatt, do you have a moment?"

Wyatt seemed to have guessed everything from the crest on the letter. Yet, to ask, "Is there something you need, My Lady?"—he really is a sly one.

"The position of lady-in-waiting to Princess Margaret. Is it impossible to decline? I don't think it's right for me."

That's right, I want to look after my younger brothers and improve life here at the Granger household.

"I believe refusing a request from Queen Victoria would be considered a lack of discretion."

In other words, I can't say no. Patience is also shouting, "How could you refuse!"

"Then, is there any merit to being a lady-in-waiting? I'm ignorant of the ways of the world, so I know nothing."

I don't know anything about this other world, and I leave the noble rules to Patience.

"My Lady, being chosen as a lady-in-waiting to Princess Margaret is an honor. They will surely provide you with appropriate clothing and belongings for the role."

If it were a noblewoman, dress costs would be expensive, and perhaps I might even receive jewelry, but my current uniform is enough for me.

More importantly, is there any pay? Even something like a part-time wage would be fine. After all, I don't know how much time I'll have to spend with Princess Margaret, but I would be sacrificing my comfortable dorm life. It's only natural to demand compensation, right? This is where I differ from Patience, who is a noble through and through. She's making a racket in my head, but I'm ignoring her!

"Wyatt, for the sake of my younger brothers, I want horses and Hanon, and I’m also thinking of hiring tutors to teach them horseback riding, swordsmanship, dancing, and music."

I indirectly pointed out that I need money. However, Wyatt didn't back down.

"My Lady, you do not know the world. If you are truly concerned about Master Nashius and Master Henry, you should respond to Queen Victoria's request. Furthermore, I humbly believe that fulfilling your duties as Princess Margaret's lady-in-waiting splendidly will brighten the future of your younger brothers."

In other words, he told me that as long as my father’s dismissal hangs over us, the future for Nashius and Henry is bleak. He's right; a viscount's children after a dismissal start from a deficit. That won't do!

I don't know how useful it will be to start as the brother of Princess Margaret’s lady-in-waiting, but Wyatt exhorted me to do a favor for Queen Victoria.

"I understand. However, I need those horses and Hanon. Please make the arrangements to have them ready."

I'll have George the manservant teach me about swords and horses. That means I don't have time to be having Omar wash things. I also need to obtain a magic stone.

"And, a magic stone too."

Wyatt bows, saying, "As you wish." He must know everything I'm thinking. I'm no match for him.
