Strange Girl... Keith Prince POV

This is a story from Prince Keith's point of view.

I am Keith, the second prince of the Lawrence Kingdom. I respect my older brother, Prince Richard. He is also the student council president of the Royal Academy.

I, too, turned 10 years old, so I thought I would be attending the Royal Academy just like my older brother, Prince Richard, and my older sister, Princess Margaret, but Father suddenly ordered me to "live in the dormitory." Until last year, my brother and sister were attending from home. The Royal Academy is right next to the Royal Palace, so it's not like they couldn't commute. Most nobles also commute from their mansions. Only lower-ranking rural nobles and commoners who passed the entrance exam live in the dorms.

I wonder why Father suddenly said such a thing, but I cannot disobey Father's orders.

"Prince Keith, I will be moving into the dorms as well."

I was happy that Ralph and Hugo, who have played with me at the Royal Palace as school friends, moved into the dorms with me. There are no personal attendants in the dorms. Putting aside class hours, it's lonely not having anyone to talk to.

Prince Richard is only staying in the dorm for one year, yet even he is moving in. When I asked if he wasn't dissatisfied with that, I was scolded.

"Father wants us to learn to be independent."

That may be true, but some of my brother's school friends also moved into the dorms—is that also considered "being independent"?

My and my brother's school friends moved into the dorms with us, but Princess Margaret's school friends did not. Apparently, it is because, unlike men, women require various forms of maintenance. In that case, what will my sister do? I feel a little sorry for her. Besides, my sister is very weak in the mornings. I know this because every morning, the court ladies and maids make a huge fuss to ensure she isn't late for school. She will be alone in the dorm. Will she be okay?

It's a nuisance that moving into the dorm takes so much time.

"They should have just moved the beds and things in beforehand," I grumbled to Ralph while waiting in the dining hall for the rooms to be prepared.

"Normal dormitory students, like that girl student over there, only have one wardrobe trunk, you see. So there probably isn't any need to carry things in beforehand."

She's a first-year student because she's small. But since those who live in the dorms are lower-ranking nobles, I figured she wouldn't be in Class A and glanced at her before forgetting about her.

『Clang, clang』

"The bell is ringing quite a lot."

"They say there are students who don't have watches, so they use the bell to tell the time."

I am dumbfounded by Ralph's words. Are there really nobles who don't own a watch? While I was dumbfounded, Hugo stood up from his seat.

"This is the bell for lunch. It seems we have to go get it ourselves. I will go and get it."

In front of the counter, several people were standing in line holding trays. I was getting fed up thinking that I would have to eat here every morning and night from now on, when I heard my brother shouting.

"Do you intend to spoil Keith and curry favor with him? Father put us in the dorms because he wants us to learn independence. Do you intend to defy his will?"

It seems Hugo did something to make my brother angry.

"Keith, come here and apologize to this girl student."

I walk over to my brother. However, I don't think there is any reason for me to be scolded.

"Brother, I did not ask for such a thing. This guy just went and did it on his own..."

I was intimidated by my brother and scolded to reflect on my actions. However, the female student I was supposed to apologize to was nowhere to be found, and my brother looked a little awkward as well. I wondered if she might be the girl who had moved into the dormitory with just a single wardrobe box earlier.

"A strange woman." My first impression of Patience Granger was not very good. That was because Hugo had cut in line, and I had been subjected to my brother's intimidation as a result. There was a bit of misplaced anger involved as well.

I ended up giving the welcoming address as the representative of the incoming students at the entrance ceremony. Naturally, my brother gave the welcoming address as the student council president. Since it was right after his magnificent speech, I was nervous. However, Ralph and Hugo praised me, saying, "That was excellent." It might have been flattery, but I felt a little relieved.

Naturally, I am in Class A. It’s a given for first-years since classes are based on social status, but from the second year onwards, they are based on grades. I absolutely must stay in Class A. Both my brother and sister have managed to keep their spots in Class A.

Self-introductions are boring. I only listened to the first few people and Louise, who is a beauty, but I was surprised that the last person, Patience Granger, was the girl who had run away. If she's from a Viscount's family, shouldn't she be commuting from her estate? It felt fitting for a shrimp like her to have been granted only "life magic." Somehow, being scolded by my brother earlier is still weighing on me.

And yet, that shrimp keeps skipping grades one after another. It’s somehow infuriating. On top of that, she even received a certificate of completion in practical magic. The instructor is an old man, so he must be lenient. It’s unfair. Even though I’m being given such intense instruction.

I achieved grade skips in mathematics, language, magic studies, dance, and music. I think that’s a pretty satisfying result, but Patience passed every single subject and skipped to the second year.

"The Granger family is a family of scholars, after all," Ralph consoled me.

I thought about leaving the bookworm shrimp alone, but for some reason, she was sitting at the same table as my brother and sister during Sunday dinner.

"Why are you here?" The shrimp didn't answer my question, so my sister did.

"Patience is my personal attendant, chosen by Mother."

If Mother chose her, I shouldn't interfere. I understand at least that much.

I told her, "Make sure you take good care of her." My sister is not a morning person.

Somehow, this Patience is able to wake my sister up on her own and even get her to eat breakfast. As expected, Mother never makes a mistake.

In the second-year language class I skipped into, Louise was enthusiastically asking Patience, "How did you become a personal attendant? Tell me!" but if she isn't prepared to live in the dormitory, it's impossible. Besides, I didn't think Louise could wake my sister up. Unlike that girl, she is a normal young lady.

However, that girl is also useful. Lunch is

Upper-class cafeteria

I am ordered by Mother to eat there with my brother and sister, but if I leave vegetables behind, I get scolded, and sometimes I end up making my brother angry. Patience, who sits at the same table as my personal attendant, has defused my brother's anger several times. I'm not really sure how she does it, but I’ve changed my opinion of her slightly.

And yet, when it comes to her, she’s blushing because that eccentric Albert proposed to her. I heard my sister turned him down, but isn't it possible that she herself thought it was a chance to marry into wealth?

"Hmph, the only one who would propose to you is an eccentric."

I said it because I was feeling somehow irritable, and although I was reflecting that it might have been a bit harsh, that woman of all people is out alone. Does she not have any sense of danger? Because she's a shrimp, does she lack the refinement expected of a lady?

"Where have you been?"

I found myself leaning out of the carriage window and shouting. And when it comes to her, she brazenly says, "I just went for a little walk." She really is a strange woman. But for some reason, she's on my mind.

I'll keep quiet about her going out. She has deflected my older brother’s anger away from me several times. I do not forget a favor.
