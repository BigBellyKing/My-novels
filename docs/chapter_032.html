I received my certificate of completion!

Wednesdays have classes in the morning, and they are exhausting.

In the advanced cafeteria

for lunch, I listened to the classical literature class seriously in the afternoon. Patience can pass, but I need to study.

Elementary history only covers the general flow of eras, but I don't know any of it.

In my previous life, we learned the general flow of history in elementary school, then learned it in a bit more detail in middle school, and in high school, we learned even more detail, like why things happened. First year of elementary school is about the level of elementary school. And second and third year of elementary school is probably about the level of middle school.

Home economics is with Louise. If she wants to be a personal maid that badly, I wish she'd just join the dormitory. Stop asking for things that are impossible.

Second-year home economics was, as expected, not just about sewing straight lines.

"For the spring semester, I'd like you to embroider a makeup cape. You who skipped grades are a little behind, but don't worry, it's fine if you finish it during the spring semester."

Ms. Cameron has a gentle way of speaking, and it's comforting. It's true that the second-years have been embroidering their makeup capes since the first week, but it's only a little bit so far.

"Are the patterns free to choose?" Louise is proactive in asking questions, so that makes things easier.

"Yes, but we will be displaying them at the end of the spring semester, so it would be embarrassing if the pattern were too simple."

Louise seemed to be fired up by the teacher's words. She immediately began drawing complex patterns on her makeup cape with chalk. I thought I might use it when brushing Princess Margaret's hair; it's a simple idea, but I drew a ring of marguerite flowers and leaves around the entire hem of the cape.

I knew I only had to finish it by the end of the spring semester, but I get absorbed in this kind of work. Before I knew it, I had finished. It seems I used life magic unconsciously along the way. I've done it now.

"Oh my, you must be Patience Granger. I'm friends with Mr. Jefferson, the life magic teacher. I'd heard about you, but you're truly something. It seems there's nothing left for me to teach you."

I was handed the certificate of completion with a "Here you go." Perhaps I have good chemistry with older teachers? Ah, I feel a burning gaze from Louise, but more than that, I'm happy that my free time has increased.

I'm now exempt from the 4th period on Mondays and Thursdays for magic practicals, and Wednesdays for home economics. I can bring socks from home and do side work, or use the time to review math, or study language, literature, history, or magic. Yay!

My studies are fine, except for dancing. I think I can do it if I practice. I'll seriously consider skipping ahead to the secondary division.

Unlike the elementary division, the secondary division is credit-based. Furthermore, it is divided into the Knight course, the Civil Officer course, the Mage course, and the Home Economics (bride training) course. I imagine Princess Margaret and her classmates will take the Home Economics (bride training) course. I have no dowry, and I want to look after my younger brothers until they can earn their own way. In other words, in this otherworldly context, I'm destined to be a spinster. Besides, when Nasios gets married, I don't want to get in the way as a sister-in-law. (Ah, just thinking about Nasios getting married makes me want to cry.)

『I have no choice but to become a working woman!』

Right, Patience said she wanted to become a lady-in-waiting, too. Well, in the Granger family, the only marriage options seem to be becoming a trophy wife for some wealthy old man. A proud person like Patience couldn't stand that. I don't have a thing for older men either, so I'd hate that.

Ah, but if it were someone who would let me spend money freely, maybe I could open a sweets shop? "Ouch!" Whoa, Patience is furious. After all, the body is Patience's. Sorry!

"Maybe I should take the civil official course." It seems Patience is in favor of it, too. Good grief, I really wish she’d stop the headache attacks.

Still, just classes on law and such would be boring. The school handbook doesn't list the curriculum requirements for each course. I suppose they’ll explain those when we advance to the secondary division.

When in doubt, go to Mr. Kepner. Since there are a lot of practical classes in the afternoon, I hope he's in the staff room.

"Oh, Patience? Wait, wasn't it your home economics period?"

When I showed him the certificate of completion, he was surprised and happy for me.

"Come to think of it, I heard you joined the music club. Surely you should be able to get a certificate for music as well. Since every member of the music club gets one."

With those members, that's entirely possible.

"I've been told I still need more practice, so I'm not sure. More importantly, what kind of curriculum does the civil official course in the secondary division entail?"

Mr. Kepner looked a little surprised, but then he nodded in understanding.

"Most girls take the home economics (bridal training) course, but the Granger family is different indeed. The civil official course is difficult, but it's rewarding."

Ah, that’s not really the noble motivation I have. I wanted to make excuses, saying I just don't want to become a spinster and a sister-in-law who gets in the way of her younger brother's married life.

"The curriculum requirements for the civil official course... ah, I only have the one for all courses, but will this do?"

I can't tell him the details of my family situation, and since I’ve obtained the curriculum requirements for the secondary division, I head back to my room.
