Spring is busy!

I went strawberry picking with my younger brothers in the greenhouse over the weekend. Since they first sprouted, I’ve been taking care of them—protecting them with straw and all—so I’m satisfied to see them growing into bright red, jewel-like strawberries. Plus, I’m happy to see Nashius and Henry shouting "delicious!" They’re seriously cute!

Learning from the mistake of giving them a sugar high with those pancakes earlier, I won't let them eat too many strawberries at once. I’d be in trouble if they got a stomachache.

"Only 5, okay? Don't eat them all at once. There you go, Henry, put them in the basket. I'll make you something delicious later."

It’s written all over their faces that they still want more, but no. Besides, I'm planning to use the strawberries not just for dessert tonight, but to make jam, too. You need preserved food, after all.

I came to this different world in winter, and it was truly tough. I want to secure plenty of preserved food before this winter comes.

Since then, I’ve been taken to the royal palace in Princess Margaret’s carriage several times on Fridays to play the Hannon exercises in front of Queen Victoria. It wears on my nerves, so I wish she’d spare me, but I get to bring home a basket full of eggs, butter, sugar, and the like, so I guess it’s fine. Plus, it saves me the carriage rental fee each time. That’s important!

Sometimes I make sweets with the eggs and butter I receive, but other times I just use them for cooking. So, I’ve been planning to make jam with the sugar I’ve saved up. I want to fill...

The pantry...

With rows and rows of preserved food.

Since it’s spring, I’m also going to make a vegetable garden in the backyard. Patience is making a fuss about how to prune the roses in the front garden and so on, but that’s on the back burner. I never want to be hungry again. Ah, it reminds me of a Civil War movie I saw a long time ago. The protagonist clutches a handful of dirt and vows, "I will never let my family go hungry again." I feel the same way.

"I will never let anyone in the Granger family go hungry again!" Not just the family, but the people who serve us despite the Grangers' poverty, too.

The backyard has been neglected and is overgrown with weeds. George has been trying to take care of the front garden so it wouldn't be embarrassing, but it's impossible for one person to manage.

"Weeds, be gone!"

I’m not sure if this spell is right, but I chant it while imagining the weeds being pulled up, leaving behind brown soil. Oh my, it’s a clean plot of land now. The weeds are all gathered in one spot. If I make a compost bin or something, I could probably make fertilizer, but I'll leave that to George.

I pick up a fallen tree branch and draw a rectangle on the ground. I plan to turn about two-thirds of the backyard into a field. Near the back fence, there are the original trees, and in front of them, I want to plant fruit trees. Apples, pears, cherries, maybe. Citrus might be impossible because it’s too cold. I saved up the money to buy seedlings and seeds by doing side jobs. But I’m worried if I’ll have time for side jobs next year. Taking two courses in secondary school seems like it will be busy.

"Is there no way to earn more money?"

I feel like I’ve been thinking only about money since I came to this different world. That’s right—in my past life, my parents worked to raise me. I never had any financial troubles. And yet, they died before me. I’m sorry.

But I have an older brother and sister at home, and both of them are married and have grandchildren. I’m the youngest, and I used to want a younger brother; I was a shotacon.

Huh? Was I chosen because there wouldn't be any real harm if I disappeared? Or was it because they knew I’d dote on the younger boys since I’m a shotacon? It seems like there are monsters in this other world, but I don't think I came here to defeat them. After all, I only have life magic.

Did I come to this other world only to help the poor Granger family? It’s the father being unemployed that's the problem, after all. I know my family must be sad. Hmph.

Getting angry won't change anything, though. I have to do what I can.

"Become a ridge!"

I'll plant basic vegetables here, like beans, potatoes, onions, and carrots. After the strawberries in the greenhouse are done, it'll be time for summer vegetables. I want to turn things like tomatoes into sauce and bottle them. Pickled cabbage can also be a preserved food. It’s going to get busy from now on.

But, I need money to buy jars and seasonings like vinegar. After all, darning socks alone pays too little. I'll have a direct negotiation with Wyatt!

"I’ve also received a certificate in art. But I want to draw more. Wouldn't it be fun to paint miniatures on tea sets and such?"

Wyatt seemed to think about it for a while, but he agreed. However, this isn't possible at the academy. I could put socks in my bag and take them with me, but pottery is heavy.

I really need to find something more profitable. I pick up old items in the attic and think about what I can do with them.

"There are plenty of curtains, so should I make them into bedspreads and sell them? Or rather, if I refurbished those old dresses, could they sell?"

I might repair and resew the old dresses to wear when I get a little bigger. Currently, the academy uniform covers almost everything, but I'll need them after I graduate. The hand-me-downs from relatives are only children's clothes. Well, that makes sense. They gave them to me because children's clothes can't be worn once they grow out of them.

"These dresses aren't Mother’s, are they? They’re from much further back."

The designs are different from the dresses I see at the royal palace; they’re much puffier and more voluminous. They’re dresses from a bygone era, but they use a lot of fabric.

I pick up a dress filled with lace and think.

"Lace is expensive, isn't it? If I make things like handkerchiefs with this, wouldn't they sell for a high price?"

Princess Margaret and my classmates have lace trimmings on their handkerchiefs. Mine doesn't. But, if I use the lace from this old dress, I might be able to make it work.

That said, the lace on the dress isn't made for trimming handkerchiefs. It has a large pattern and isn't long and thin. But the thread is the same, right?

"Become thread!" I try turning it into thread for a moment.

"Become a thin lace trim!" I chant, while picturing the lace-knitted border on Princess Margaret’s handkerchief.

"Ah, I’m quite tired."

Dozens of meters of lace-knitted border spun out. It was many times more tiring than making ridges in the field. It must be because it’s such detailed work. I clean up an empty box, carefully wind the lace around it, and head back to my room. All that’s left is to rope in my accomplice, Mary, and make handkerchiefs with lace borders.

Handkerchiefs with lace trim were more profitable than darning socks. Besides, this is something I can do as a side job even in the dorm. Nice!
