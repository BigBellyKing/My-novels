Home

I'm going to cry.

After that, I also mended my younger brothers' clothes, and my father's as well, of course. And the curtains in the room we use, too. It gets cold when the curtains are thin and worn out.

Speaking of thin, I also mended the thin futon, and it puffed up just a little bit. Tsk, it was thin to begin with. Googling Patience's knowledge, it seems you're supposed to put a luxurious bedspread over it. I can't find anything like that.

"If you can't find it, you should just make one."

I grabbed Mary, who had become my accomplice by now, and after coaxing her through her fussing about how this was no place for a young lady to be, I had her take me to the attic.

I only stated a fact when I said, "It's so dusty," but Mary looked apologetic. I’m the one who should be sorry. We’re probably the only house where the master makes the maid do side jobs. I’m sure of it.

"Become clean!" Oh my, how wonderful. The attic, covered in dust and grime, started to look like an atelier in Paris. That might be an exaggeration, but now I can concentrate on looking for things. Even if I get covered in dust, I can clean myself instantly with life magic, but I can't stop sneezing otherwise.

"This is quite a beautiful curtain."

Old curtains from when the family was well-off, worn-out carpets, and old clothes that have gone out of style. To me, it's a mountain of treasure.

"I'm surprised you kept these."

If they had sold them, they could have made at least a little money... I don't know if they forgot about them or thought these old things wouldn't sell. My father lacks life skills way too much.

Well, there's no use complaining about that. First, I'll mend the curtains.

"Hey, we can make bedspreads out of these, can't we?"

Together with Mary, I sew bedspreads for myself, my brothers, and my father. Sewing with life magic is fast, too. This is really convenient.

"Mary, do you think I should become a lady-in-waiting at the royal palace?"

I ask Mary while sewing bedspreads with life magic. In Patience's memories, she said she "wanted to be a lady-in-waiting at the royal palace," but personally, the world of women is scary, so I’d rather avoid it.

"Becoming a lady-in-waiting at the royal palace is very difficult. First, you must have good grades at the academy, and your family background is also questioned. Of course, the Viscount Granger family background is no problem."

Mary asserts with confidence that the Viscount Granger family is no problem, but looking at this extreme poverty, there is a huge problem. I also want to know why my father was fired.

While listening to Mary talk as she sews, I finally realized that a lady-in-waiting and a maid are different. It seems a lady-in-waiting serves as an attendant, secretary, and advisor to the Queen or Princess. However, it seems they have similar jobs as well. There are ladies-in-waiting who manage wardrobes, help choose accessories, and style hair. It seems Mary isn't very familiar with royal ladies-in-waiting either. Although she knows a hundred times more than I do.

Before going to the academy, there is something I must do for my brothers: improve their meals. I've finished repairing the rooms we use. I'll just do the rest slowly during summer vacation.

"Eva... she's thin, isn't she?"

Even though chefs are usually imagined as being overweight, Eva is thin. Plus, she’s almost never in the kitchen. Well, not that she prepares very elaborate meals anyway, but I wonder where she is?

In times like these, the best thing to do is ask Mary, my accomplice. Because Wyatt the butler seems like a tough nut to crack, and George the footman looks genuinely busy. One moment he’s lighting the fireplace, the next he’s putting it out, only to light it again. That’s not all—he’s also washing the chamber pots Mary collected and cleaning the garden. He doesn't seem like he has the time to answer questions.

Mary is just as busy, but she comes to help me change before dinner. I can catch her while she’s brushing my hair.

"Hey, where is Eva usually?"

Mary was startled and dropped her brush.

"I believe Eva is in the kitchen. She has to prepare dinner, after all."

She’s agitated! That means she’s up to something. Mary has been doing side work, but that happens in the maid's quarters. Eva spends a lot of time away from the kitchen. So, she must be going out somewhere.

"Does she work somewhere?"

I feign innocence to probe her.

"No, of course not..." She’s extremely rattled. Let me push a little further.

"By any chance, is the ham on our table something Eva earns by working?"

If I recall correctly, ham should be expensive. We eat it regularly in modern times, but I remember reading in a book that there was an era when commoners couldn't even afford it.

"Please keep this a secret from the Viscount."

Hearing that Eva works at the market from early in the morning and receives ham as payment instead of wages made me dizzy with guilt. That’s why breakfast is always just pre-made soup and hard bread. When I think of Eva coming home before noon and slicing that precious ham so thin, so thin, it brings me to tears.

"I have to do something about this."

I am living thanks to my servants. But that is wrong.

"I want to earn money. But side work alone isn't enough... Besides, once I go to the academy, I can only do side work on weekends, and it costs money to get back home."

I was planning on having Mary pick me up at the academy on Saturday morning so we could walk home. Since making Mary walk the round trip would be exhausting, I need to put her on a hackney carriage for at least the way there. Will she not ride it on the way back? Noble young ladies don't ride hackney carriages. However, taking a walk with a lady's maid is acceptable. Apparently, it's fine even if it's a long walk. When I heard from Mary that it’s trendy for ladies who need to diet to take walks in the park, I felt like killing someone. They must be eating sweet cakes or something!

I’ll walk to the academy on Sunday afternoon, and have Mary return to the mansion by hackney carriage. In other words, I need money for a round-trip hackney carriage fare. My plan was to cover that with side work. But I can't leave Eva as she is, and I also need to improve our meals.
