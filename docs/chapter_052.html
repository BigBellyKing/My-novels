The South Gate of the Royal Capital, Romano

"Nashius, Henry, take care!"

I wave to my younger brothers who are seeing me off from the carriage sent by the Royal Palace. I can't lean out of the window because Mary will scold me, so they soon disappear from view.

"We are scheduled to meet up with everyone else's carriages at the South Gate."

Lady-in-waiting Charlotte is also riding in this carriage. But since Queen Victoria and Princess Margaret aren't here, I can relax.

"I have never left the royal capital before."

Patience has no memories of anywhere other than the royal capital either. Or rather, other than going to the church for her mother's funeral, she has never stepped outside the estate. A true sheltered girl.

"Then you have never seen the sea either, have you? It will be fun."

That’s right, it will be fun! If it’s near the sea, the fish will probably be delicious, too. Since I'm even separated from my younger brothers, I have to have something to look forward to, or I won't be able to stand it. As I think about picking up pretty seashells to bring back as souvenirs for them, it seems we have passed through the aristocratic district.

"Mary, there are shops."

There are many shops, but it’s still early in the morning, so they aren't open yet. Still, I found signs that look like clothing and accessory stores.

"My lady, Romano has every kind of shop imaginable. Now, please sit properly."

Just for bringing my face close to the window, I was scolded by Mary. Since I am going to the summer palace with Queen Victoria, Mary's manners check has become stricter. Even though she was more lenient when we were accomplices.

The houses gradually become smaller, and the atmosphere becomes a bit more crowded.

"Ah, there are street stalls."

Stalls were open for the people who work from early in the morning.

"Those are adventurers. They likely receive jobs at the guild and are grabbing a bite to eat before leaving through the gate."

Adventurer's Guild! That’s such an "otherworld" word. I wonder if there are any part-time jobs, like cleaning. But even just going to a shop is impossible for me right now, so that's a long way off.

"Lady Charlotte, you really know so many things."

Patience really is ignorant of the world, and I only know what I learned at the academy about this other world.

"I did pass the lady-in-waiting exam, after all."

So there's an exam for ladies-in-waiting. I should make a note of that. It feels like there are few places for women to work in this other world. Especially for noble daughters, it seems normal to just get married. The Granger family has no dowry. Well, even without a dowry, I could still marry. For example, to an upstart who wants a noble name, or a widower who lost his wife and needs someone to help raise his children. Neither Patience nor I want that!

"Um, I would like to choose the civil official course; would that be advantageous for anything?"

It's a rare chance to be in the same carriage as an active lady-in-waiting. I should ask!

"My, you are choosing the civil official course? I chose the housekeeping course and earned credits in subjects like law and administration. Students who graduate from the civil official course take the lower-level bureaucracy exam to become officials, but I wonder about women. You would likely be able to pass the lady-in-waiting exam, though."

It seems there are no female bureaucrats. As I feel disappointed, Charlotte-san encourages me.

"However, there are female knights. They are particularly active as guards for female royalty. So, please do not give up, even if there are no female bureaucrats now."

While we were talking about that, it seems we arrived at the South Gate. The Royal Palace's carriages haven't arrived yet, but many knights were already lined up.

"Oh, it's Lord Eugene. He is the female knight we were just talking about."

It was no wonder that Charlotte, who was usually calm, seemed a bit flustered. Lord Eugene was truly wonderful. He shined like the top star of a girl's opera troupe from my previous life.

"He is a very wonderful person, isn't he?"

"Isn't he? And he is very strong, too. He won the championship in the Knight Club tournament."

I was surprised, though I suppose it’s only natural for someone who chose the Knight course. It would be impossible for me, who can't even catch the horse standing quietly by my side.

"If Lord Eugene is here, the Queens will arrive soon as well."

Prompted by Charlotte, I got off the carriage and waited for their arrival.

"The South Gate is quite large, isn't it?"

Not only is the height impressive, but the width is incredible too.

"Please stop looking around."

Mary tugged on my sleeve without a word. It’s a light green, summery dress that was finally finished yesterday.

I attached white lace trim, which I made using life magic, to the collar, cuffs, and hem. Unlike the handkerchief, it took a long time to sew it on, and it was hard work. I especially worried about whether the hem of the skirt would ever be finished. Since it's puffed out slightly with a petticoat, the hem was extremely long.

Mary also braided ribbons made of the same fabric into my hair. Mary put a lot of effort into getting me ready from the morning. In other words, I was woken up incredibly early. Standing here seriously, I’m bored and a little sleepy. We've been waiting quite a while—I wonder when they'll be here?

"They seem to be a little late, but they have arrived."

A long line of carriages, led by knights, is approaching. It's such a long procession of carriages that I find myself thinking, "Did I even need to come?"

The lead carriage stopped, so I remained bowing without raising my head.

"Patience, I'm glad you came. Now, off you go."

I thought we were departing, but Princess Margaret is pleading with the Queen.

"I want to ride with Patience."

Huh? But I’d prefer the company of Lady-in-Waiting Charlotte and Mary—they're easy to be around.

"I suppose it can't be helped. If you wish to ride with your lady-in-waiting, then so be it."

Princess Margaret stepped down gracefully.
