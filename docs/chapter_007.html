Got a blanket!

I returned to my room and, while shivering from the cold, read "Elementary Life Magic" and "On Estenaism," two of the thinner books I found in the library.

There were rows of books that looked more professional on the top shelves, but they looked difficult, so I avoided them. Born in Japan, I had never had magic. As for religion, one moment we’d be having a Christmas party, the next we’d be ringing the bell on New Year’s Eve, and then visiting a shrine for the first prayer of the new year. I had only heard a little bit about religion during ethics class.

"It's so cold, I can't even turn the pages."

I thought it might be warmer if I crawled into bed, but Patience's etiquette check kicked in, and I gave up on that idea.

"Elementary Life Magic doesn't tell me much more than what Patience already knows. Well, it's for children, so I suppose that can't be helped. I'll leave it until tomorrow, once I know if I can use life magic."

I learned that the magic seems quite useful. If I can use it, it looks like I might be able to improve my living conditions a bit. Especially with things like the toilet. Yes, right now it's a chamber pot. Mary cleans it diligently, but having a chamber pot is just wrong.

Searching through my memories, I recall that the Granger household does have a toilet. But it seems you need a magic stone to use it. Why do I know something like that? It’s been a few days since I became Patience, and today was the first day I’ve gotten up and walked around the manor, but what comes out must come out. How embarrassing!

At first, I thought perhaps I was using a chamber pot in my room because my body was weak. I really felt like I was dying, and I had to have Mary help me get out of bed... sigh, I’ve thrown away all my shame. But that time it was number one, so I somehow managed to get through it.

Then, yesterday, it was finally number two. Using the chamber pot for that is just... yeah. So, I rang the bell by the bedside to call Mary, who must be busy.

"Mary, sorry to bother you, but could you take me to the toilet?"

That was when I was told that the house's toilet couldn't be used.

"There is no magic stone, so..." she said apologetically, but I felt more apologetic than she did. There is only Mary as a maid, and she is taking care of not just a sick person, but everything else. I'm sorry to be such a mistress.

Well, life magic also includes cleaning, and if I can use that, it seems I could even flush the toilet. Aiming for a civilized life!!

But I can't stay in the toilet forever. First of all, I want to be able to use the toilet myself. After all, even though I'm 10 years old, I'm a maiden who should be bashful about such things.

"Oh, speaking of improving my living conditions, I have to do something about this thin futon."

I suddenly looked up and realized it was getting dark. It seems that because we are at a high latitude, the sun sets early in winter. Of course, the poor Granger household has candles, but Patience's memory keeps warning me that I have to save them.

I don't want to wake up from the cold again tonight. I'll go ask Mary if there's anything else besides this thin futon.

"If I remember correctly, Mary should be... the kitchen and the servant's workroom are on the semi-basement floor."

Patience's memory is fuzzy on whether or not this is a place for a noble lady to go. But it seems I have been there a few times. Remembering the time my mother and I made snacks together when she was still alive, my stomach let out a hungry growl.

"So the custom of tea has been cut and done away with."

It’s no use getting angry and saying, "But isn't the nobility supposed to be all dressed up and having tea parties!" At the current Granger household, there is no such thing as tea time. Perhaps they don't even have the money to buy tea leaves.

By the way, the servants' sleeping quarters are on the third floor.

In the semi-basement, there is the butler's study, the housekeeper's room—though she isn't here right now—the kitchen, and the maid's room. That maid's room is quite spacious, isn't it? Even though there’s only Mary living there.

"Mary, do you have a blanket or something?"

I ask Mary, who was resewing my outdoor clothes.

"My lady, in a place like this... as for a blanket..."

I didn't mean to trouble Mary. They must have sold off any extra bedding.

"Let's see. If it's a servant's blanket... but that wouldn't be appropriate for you, my lady."

What? So you have one!

"It's better than being cold. I was nearly at death's door from pneumonia, you know."

I follow Mary, who holds a large iron ring with multiple keys on it, to the linen room.

"To think of using something so unrefined..."

Mary balked, saying it wasn't becoming of a viscount's daughter, but I am overjoyed to have gotten my hands on a gray blanket.

I asked about "the younger brothers too...", but Mary decided that children shouldn't be cold, and since they are boys, it would be fine even if it were something unrefined; it seems they were already using blankets.

I also had her take one out for Father, who seems to have no ability to provide for himself. Even in such poverty, the reason we are somehow scraping by seems to be because of support from Father's relatives. Besides, if we became orphans,

my younger brothers

would be pitiable, wouldn't they?

"For his lordship the Viscount... I suppose so. He seems unable to sleep due to the cold."

Exactly! That's it! You can't just worry about appearances.

I feel like I'll be able to sleep soundly tonight.
