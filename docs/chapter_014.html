I’m going to move into the Royal Academy.

I don't know what kind of high price Wyatt sold the restored antiques for, but Eva no longer needs to go to the market to work. Furthermore, we can now buy meat normally, and with the harvest of vegetables and beans from the greenhouse, our dietary situation has improved.

That said, it’s still simple fare. The soup is full of ingredients and seasoned. The bread has become a bit softer and thicker. Besides ham, there are more days when grilled meat is served. And since the vegetables are home-grown, they are served steamed or sautéed as a side dish.

"It looks like we won't be starving anymore."

I can enter the dormitory with peace of mind. I don't have enough to afford a horse yet, so it's just for weekend rentals. But, big sister will be back on the weekends! Saying goodbye to my father was quick, but saying goodbye to my younger brothers couldn't be done without tears.

"Big sister, take care." Nashius, it’s not like I’m going that far... you're going to make me cry.

"Big sister, are you really coming back on the weekend?" ...Henry, of course. I’ll come back even if it rains cats and dogs.

"You two, take care of yourselves."

Wiping away my tears, I board the carriage. Of course, Mary is with me. This lady's maid system is incredibly inconvenient. I’d like to leave the academy and go to the downtown area for a bit, but I wonder if that's impossible? I might find a way to make money other than odd jobs.

The carriage rattles through the noble district. Somewhere in this mansion might be the relative who gave me the Royal Academy uniform I'm wearing. No one approaches the Viscount Granger family, who are currently removed from their posts. I'm very anxious about my future school life. I'll keep a low profile. While I was thinking this, the royal palace came into view.

"My, it's so large." Good thing I used my "Patience" filter. If I'd said "It's huge!", Mary would have been surprised.

So this is where my father worked. I wonder what kind of mistake he made that led to his dismissal. Next time Wyatt asks me to restore a broken antique, I'll ask him about it discreetly. Though, he seems like a tough nut to crack.

The royal palace was too vast. If the Royal Academy had been in front of it, I might have been able to walk there every day, but getting past the royal palace looks exhausting.

"That over there is the Royal Academy."

Told by Mary, I am surprised, thinking it's a second royal palace. Well, it's smaller than the royal palace, but it's much bigger than my image of a school. I'm afraid I'll get lost; will I be okay?

I immediately understood why the gates and the forecourt had to be so wide. That's right, there was a traffic jam of carriages.

"Today is supposed to be only for students moving into the dorms, right? I wonder if they're struggling with unloading their luggage?"

That's right, for those commuting, tomorrow's entrance ceremony would have been fine. I want that day I spent with my brothers back. Poverty is just awful, isn't it?

"Students from local noble families who do not have a residence in Romano will be moving into the dorms, my lady."

Hmm, so even among the local nobility, wealthy students don't live in the dorms. In other words, are the dorms only for poor students? I had thought maybe it would be a bit easier to live there, but as the carriage moves forward, the commotion somehow gives me an uneasy feeling.

"It seems everyone is bringing a lot of luggage."

The amount of luggage being unloaded from the carriages is incredible. Beds, tables—it’s like they’re moving house. I only have one wardrobe trunk and a wooden box for my school supplies. It’s not that I’ve “packed compactly,” I just don’t have anything else. I hate being poor! But the academy requires uniforms, so I don’t see why they’d need so much luggage, do you?

“Could that be…?”

Mary, please don't just reach a conclusion on your own. She’s staring at the carriages, getting all excited and saying, “What shall we do! What shall we do!”

“They’re all incredibly fancy carriages, but shouldn't they be able to commute in these?”

It’s a nuisance for rich people to barge into a dormitory life meant for poor nobles. Besides, this traffic jam is all because of their excessive luggage. I was fuming, but then Mary grabbed my hand firmly.

“My lady, please behave yourself. I can’t believe princes and princesses are going to be staying in the dormitory. So that’s why the children of prestigious families are moving in as well.”

Ugh, how troublesome. Why would such high-and-mighty people live in a dormitory? Well, I suppose their entourages of nobles are moving in too, so it has nothing to do with me.

I search through Patience’s memories. Let’s see… King Alfred of the Lawrence Kingdom has a 15-year-old Prince Richard, a 12-year-old Princess Margaret, a 10-year-old Prince Keith, an 8-year-old Princess Jane, and a 6-year-old Prince Marcus. Queen Victoria really gave it her all. It seems there are no concubines, so she’s given birth to five children in almost two years each. Clap, clap! Bravo, big mother of a large family!

“Why would the princes and princesses be in the dormitory? It wasn't like this before, was it?”

“Indeed, I have never heard of them staying in the dormitory.”

Is it a whim of King Alfred’s, like wanting them to know how the common folk live? Or perhaps he is punishing them because they were too extravagant and selfish? Either way, it’s nothing but a nuisance.

“I wonder if they can bring attendants and maids to the dormitory?”

Since I only have Mary, it’s impossible for me from the start, but I’m pretty sure the rules didn’t say anything about being able to bring maids to the dormitory.

“Well, I wonder if we should think of them the same as regular students?”

Royalty might get special treatment, but what about the nobles in their entourage? I thought about such laid-back things while waiting for the carriages to finish unloading. Complaining at a time like this wouldn’t do any good, after all. I won’t go against the bigwigs.

However, if it takes too long, the horse rental fee will end up costing more.
