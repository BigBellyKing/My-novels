Spring Semester Grades

The end-of-semester test results were posted. It seems that protection of personal information does not exist in this other world.

"Patience, you got almost a perfect score, didn't you?"

"Princess Margaret, you also have an excellent score."

Since we are outside the room, I am addressing her as Princess.

"Yes, apart from mathematics and home economics, my grades are somehow good. I hope Mother won't scold me..."

I had completely forgotten, but the home economics projects were also on display. The first-year project was fabric that looked like sewing practice. The second-year project was a makeup cape. It seems Louise ran out of time. Taking the works home is prohibited. If we brought them home, they would become homework for the maids, after all.

The third-year project was embroidery on an apron. Princess Margaret's is... at a level that makes me worry if she can graduate. Queen Victoria's eyes when she saw this were scary. Yeah, I'll decide to use my makeup cape at home. It might be taken as sarcastic, and Princess Margaret has a lovely makeup cape, after all.

The works of the middle school students were a mix of gems and stones. Fourth-year students made dresses, fifth-year students made gowns, and sixth-year students made long gowns. There are some lovely ones, but there are also ones that don't look wearable at all. Is it because the "gem" students receive their certificates and leave that the "stones" feel more numerous?

Art is also on display. My painting has a blue ribbon on it, and there are a few other works with ribbons as well.

"You are dexterous with your hands. You should practice the lute as well as Hanon. If you do that, you can play in an ensemble with my Hanon."

I don't think Princess Margaret's painting sense is bad. However, the quality is such that I wish she would pour even half of her enthusiasm for music into it. The feeling of cutting corners is immense.

Prince Keith, Ralph, Hugo, and Louise also had good grades. As expected, is Ralph being careful not to get higher grades than Prince Keith? But that is not my problem. I think Prince Keith should notice and scold him.

In my private meeting, I consulted with Mr. Kepner about receiving certificates of completion for language, classics, history, and magic studies.

"Patience, you don't have to work that hard."

I explained the situation to Mr. Kepner, who was dumbfounded.

"I intended to choose the civil service course if I skipped a grade to middle school, but since Princess Margaret is choosing the home economics course, it looks like I will be taking two courses. If the required subjects are exempted, I should be able to have some free time."

Mr. Kepner seemed surprised.

"Such a thing—there is no need to take the home economics course just because you are a lady-in-waiting. Hmm? Is it true that Patience is exempted from all practical subjects except dance... In that case, it might be possible. I will give you the textbooks for the third year and middle school for language, classics, history, and magic studies. Study them during the summer vacation."

I really wanted to spend my summer vacation playing with my younger brothers and studying. As I was complaining while returning to the dormitory carrying the heavy pile of textbooks, I was found by the bothersome Prince Keith.

"Patience, I heard you also got a certificate of completion in mathematics. Wait, are you thinking of getting certificates of completion for all subjects with those textbooks?"

I wish he would leave me alone.

"If possible, I would like to."

For some reason, Prince Keith glared at me.

"I won't lose! Next year, I will also skip a grade and become a third-year student. And the following year, I will skip to the second year of middle school. I will catch up to you."

I send my silent encouragement, thinking "Good luck with the classics you're not good at!" I was going to just bow in silence and leave because it felt like saying anything would just make him angry, but then he said, "I'll carry it for you!"

It’s unwanted meddling, but helpful. Of course, Prince Keith was the one who suggested it, but it was Ralph and Hugo who actually carried it for me. It seems one cannot have a prince carry things.

Report cards are sent to the guardians. My father was surprised by my grades and praised me. That was fine, but there was another letter on the desk. It’s déjà vu.

"It seems we are departing for the Summer Palace in one week. It says they will come to pick me up early in the morning."

It would only trouble them if I said I wanted to stay here. I should consult with Wyatt regarding the maid.

I leave the study and head toward Wyatt's office.

"My lady, what is the matter?"

He always pretends not to know anything, even though he knows everything.

"Father told me that we are departing for the Summer Palace in a week. Queen Victoria said I could bring a maid, but Lady-in-waiting Charlotte said they would assign me a maid even if I didn't bring one. So, I will be fine on my own."

Wyatt was silent for a moment, then disagreed.

"The Viscount told me to accompany Mary. For a young lady to travel without an attendant is a breach of etiquette. Even if there are dozens of ladies-in-waiting and maids at the Summer Palace, a young lady of the Granger family must be accompanied by a maid of the Granger family."

"But Mary is the only maid we have."

Wyatt doesn't even pay mind to my rebuttal.

"The only ones left at the mansion will be the men. I take care of the Viscount on a daily basis. George and Eva will take care of your younger brothers."

I can use the restroom on my own now, and unlike winter, I don't need to light the fireplace. But I want George to take care of the fields.

"My lady, please do not worry about the mansion."

He laughed as he brought out three broken antiques. He really is a tricky one.

"More importantly, you and Mary must prepare for the trip."

Being sent away with a smile, I told Mary, "We are going to the Summer Palace in a week." Mary? You're still breathing, right?

"Why didn't you tell me such an important thing sooner... no, this is no time to be saying that. I have to fix the clothes I set aside for when you grew up."

Mary held her breath for a moment, but after that, she was amazing. I used life magic until I was exhausted as well.

We desperately sewed together, resizing several sets of hand-me-downs from a female relative that I had restored to like-new condition.

"Mary, the Queen said she would provide clothes and necessities. Besides, I can clean them with life magic, so there's no need to wash them."

Exhausted, I asked Mary if it was enough yet.

"My lady, clothes provided by the Queen are just that—clothes. It is fine to gratefully accept them, but you must not rely on them."

The Granger family may be poor, but our servants are proud. The time I spend teaching my brothers in the morning is the only oasis for my heart.
