Ability check at the church

Thanks to the blanket, I was able to sleep through the night without waking up, even with the Patience-style sleeping posture. Or rather, I wonder if I'm unable to stop sleeping this way. Somehow, my thoughts are becoming more refined as well. I wonder if I'm being encroached upon by Patience.

"Still, I have to do something about it."

I'm relieved that the enthusiasm to improve the Granger family's living conditions still remains. Is this an otherworld reincarnation? Even if it's possession, isn't it quite strange?

As usual, I eat thin soup and bread, and wait for Mary in my room. After all, this seems to be an important task for Mary as a maid. It would be unpleasant if she only had the work of a housemaid.

"My lady, sorry to have kept you waiting."

Mary arrived with a blue dress. She must have altered the size.

She compliments me, saying, "It suits you," but I feel like the dress is wearing me instead. She took in the shoulders and such, but overall it's too big.

"Let's decorate your hair with a matching ribbon made from the same fabric as this dress, too."

As she happily places the ribbon made of blue fabric on the vanity, she carefully combs my hair. Since I'm a child, I don't put my hair up. It’s close to the half-up style I did last night, but she braids parts of the hair she lifts; Mary really has a good touch.

"I wish Lady Julianne could have seen you too..."

Adorning my hair with the blue ribbon, Mary looks satisfied and tears up. Apparently, Mary had been serving as a maid before my mother was married. But it's not really worth such praise. Patience's raw material isn't bad, but she's just too skin and bones.

"Now, we mustn't keep the Viscount waiting."

I put on a tight coat. Mary seems frustrated that the dress she went through the trouble of preparing is being ruined, but I’m relieved that it’s finally warm.

"Come to think of it, I've never gone out through the front entrance before."

This is my first time going out since becoming Patience. Wyatt opens the front door, which is magnificent and hard to believe belongs to someone poor. After going down a few steps, there is a carriage! Even though they are so poor, do they have a carriage?

Memories of my father going out by carriage every day back when he worked at the royal palace come back to me. The carriage had the Granger family crest—a quill and a book—on it. It's a crest fitting for the Granger family, isn't it?

"Now, Patience, get in."

Escorted by my father, it's my first carriage ride. My father is truly polite, which is a high point, but I suppress a sigh, wishing he just had some practical skills to make a living. Mary, as my lady's maid, also rides in the carriage. Apparently, it is standard for a young lady to be accompanied by a maid.

In the Granger household, where there are few servants, there is no coachman to operate the carriage. George, the manservant, is wearing a coachman's uniform, unlike usual, and drives the carriage.

I knew from Patience's memories that we lived in the royal capital, Romano. The townscape seems to be a noble district, with large mansions lined up.

I think, couldn't we live a little better if we sold the Granger mansion and bought a small house in a more commoner-friendly neighborhood? But it doesn't seem like something a daughter can comment on, like selling the family mansion. I have to improve our living conditions with what I can do. Regarding this, I’ve started to understand even without Patience’s "No!" attacks.

The church was a solemn grey building, covered entirely in intricate carvings on the outside. Although I only skimmed "On the Estena Religion" yesterday, the two figures standing at the front must surely be famous saints. Mary helps me take off my coat. It's a tight fit, so it’s hard to remove. Mary smooths out my wrinkled dress with her hands. Meanwhile, Father also takes off his coat and hands it to Mary. It seems Mary will be waiting near the entrance.

"Now, let's go in," Father says, escorting me into the church.

"Oh, it's beautiful!"

The morning sun streams in through the stained glass, filling the interior with blue, green, and yellow light, quite unlike the solemnity of the exterior.

"If you had the ability of light, you could become a nun."

A nun? That doesn't really ring a bell, and I don't want to be one, but is he perhaps worried about not being able to pay a dowry? From now on, I have to provide an education for my younger brothers as well. Money is necessary. I was thinking that I don't even need to go to an academy, while we walk through the church.

"Viscount Granger, thank you for coming."

It seems the butler had contacted them in advance, as someone who appeared to be a monk was waiting in front of the altar.

"Father Johann, this is my eldest daughter, Patience. I would like you to perform an ability assessment."

The collar and sleeves of his long white robe are embroidered with gold thread. Frankly speaking, he looks wealthier than our household.

From the side, a monk wearing a simple robe without any embroidery rolls a cart over. On the cart is a black velvet cloth, and a single disc is placed on it with great care.

"Now, please place your hand on the disc."

Prompted by Father Johann and encouraged by my father saying, "Patience, just stay calm and do it," I place my hand on the disc.

A sensation like a faint electric shock, a slight tingling, runs from my hand through my entire body.

Looking closely, there are several jewels—or perhaps magic stones—attached to the disc. Only one is glowing.

"Lady Patience has been blessed with life magic."

It's not really an ability worthy of being called "blessed," but in the Estena religion, magic is supposed to be a gift from God. I felt a little disappointed that there wasn't a "cheat" ability.

"Thank you very much."

To my surprise, Father doesn't just thank him; he pays the monk in gold coins! I've never seen gold coins before. I can't help but think, was it really necessary to come to the church for this? But I was happy to have my use of life magic officially recognized. Now I can use the toilet!
