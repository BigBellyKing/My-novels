I want to try going out into the city.

The lace-trimmed handkerchiefs were more profitable than darning socks, but that only fueled my desires.

"Without knowing what life is like in this other world, I have no way of knowing what will be profitable."

Since coming to this other world, I have only ever been to the house, the church once, the academy, and the royal palace a few times. As for the royal palace... on the way there, I just walk behind Princess Margaret, and on the way back, I'm just guided by a maid and ride in a carriage. I know that meeting the Queen isn't something that happens very often, but it's not that.

"I want to go to the city more!"

The mansion and the academy are both vast. But you know, in my previous life, I went wherever I wanted freely. I feel suffocated by the sense of confinement, like a bird in a cage. I’m sure it’s because it’s spring now. In the winter, I didn't want to move from in front of the fireplace.

"I wonder if there's anything I can do about the maid system?"

Even in the poor Granger household, Mary accompanies me whenever I go out.

"If I ask, Mary brings back seedlings and seeds quite readily. There are shops within walking distance, after all. Besides, I also go out to sell handkerchiefs."

I don't have any money, so I can't go shopping, but I want to see the city. After all, it's not in Patience's memories either. Research is necessary!

Even with such enthusiasm, the city remained far away. Not in terms of distance, but it is difficult for a young lady to go out into the city.

Just then, I happened to overhear some girls chattering in the dormitory.

"That ribbon is lovely. As expected, the royal capital has many good shops."

"That's true, they're things you can't get in the countryside."

A-class girls basically don't live in the dormitory. That means they are likely B-class or C-class girls. They are probably daughters of lower-ranking regional nobles or wealthy families, but since they don't have mansions in Romano, they live in the dorms. In other words, they are going out even though they shouldn't have maids.

What a fool I've been! I was bound by Patience's common sense. The school gate is not locked. It was just me being shackled by the maid system. Patience is screaming "No!", but I persuade her that I want to go to the city.

"I need to go out into the city and investigate what is selling well." My younger brothers will need horses. And I want them to learn swordsmanship properly, too. Especially since Henry looks like he might take the knight course. Horsemanship is mandatory for the knight course. It seems they even bring their own horses, and I'll have to buy armor and such. I intend to be graduated and working by then, but I want to send Nasius to university. One can never have enough money. My headache subsided.

"The days to go out into the city are Monday, Wednesday, or Friday."

I make a plan while looking at the schedule. Tuesday and Thursday are no good because of the music club. If I'm late, Princess Margaret will scold me. On Mondays and Wednesdays, I can only go out during the 4th period. That’s because I have to serve Princess Margaret in her room after school. But a little delay should be fine. I could probably get away with apologizing and saying I was in the library.

"The best time is Friday, when Princess Margaret returns to the royal palace. Princess Margaret has already earned her music certificate, so she leaves after the 3rd period ends."

I had been taken to the royal palace several times, but not every time. Besides, they had at least told me by the day before. Though that was just so they could make me practice Hannon's new pieces.

"If it's Friday, I just need to be back at the dormitory by dinner."

Since I wasn't told I was going to the royal palace on Thursday either, I decided on Friday.

Still

at the upper-class cafeteria

I am eating with Prince Richard and Prince Keith. I think it's about time I stopped, but it's Queen Victoria's order, so it can't be helped. Surely, once Prince Richard graduates at the end of the year, I'll be eating separately, right? Ah, but having meals with Princess Margaret's school friends seems like it would require a lot of tact as well. The reason I'm thinking about such things is that, as usual, Prince Keith is upsetting Prince Richard.

Prince Keith is really good at stepping on landmines. This isn't the time to be impressed. Princess Margaret is signaling with her eyes for me to "do something." In the three months we've been here, how many times have I had to mediate their lunch arguments... Let me see, what landmine did he step on this time? It's the Green Leaf Festival. As he mentioned in his self-introduction, Prince Keith is in the "Knight Club." Yet, he's complaining that he can't participate in the matches at the Green Leaf Festival because he's in the elementary division, and he's pestering Prince Richard, asking him to do something about it since he's the student council president.

"The management of clubs is decided by their heads. Don't you understand that the student council can't just throw its weight around?"

He is seriously angry. Prince Richard isn't wrong. He is quite admirable. But, surely we could try to understand Prince Keith's dissatisfaction too.

I signal to Princess Margaret with my eyes that it's impossible for me to speak up, but she won't let me off the hook.

It's scary to talk to Prince Richard, so I'll talk to Princess Margaret instead.

"What will be held at the Green Leaf Festival?"

"For the music club, we'll be holding recitals of new pieces in the auditorium three times a day. Also, since there are performances by the drama club and the chorus club in the auditorium, I'm looking forward to it."

"My friend Louise is in the chorus club. I wonder if first-years can participate too?"

"Well, I don't know much about the chorus club. If Louise and the others are good, wouldn't they be able to stand on stage? You are a second-year student, and yet you're presenting four new pieces, aren't you?"

With our leisurely conversation, the voltage of Prince Richard's anger seemed to have lowered.

"Louise said she would be performing in the chorus club's recital."

It's a good thing Prince Keith's attention was diverted.

"Did Patience write four new pieces?" I was praised by Prince Richard. Please pay more attention to your younger brother than to me.

"That's right, Albert was so enamored with Patience's talent that he proposed to her. Of course, I flatly refused on her behalf."

I turn bright red, thinking that she really didn't need to share that story here.

"That eccentric Albert proposed?"

Prince Richard is laughing out loud, so while diverting his attention was a success, I've been put to great shame.

"Hmph, the only one who would propose to you is an eccentric," he says—stop stepping on landmines again!
