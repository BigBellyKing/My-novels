Diplomats are cool, but what do they actually do?

On Mondays, I have Diplomacy and World History. Ever since Percival said he wanted to become a diplomat, I’ve been incredibly interested. But what does a diplomat in this fantasy world actually do? In my previous life, even ordinary people traveled abroad often, so they dealt with protecting their citizens, issues with trade deficits, and... oh, right, there were territorial issues too.

Unlike Japan, the Lawrence Kingdom is not an island nation. In other words, it shares land borders with neighboring countries, and as I learned in history, it was often at war. Diplomacy seems like it would be tough during wartime, wouldn’t it?

For some reason, Phillips is walking me to the Diplomacy classroom again today. I’ve definitely memorized where the classroom is by now. Well, I guess it’s because we’re in the same class?

"Are you going to Romano University, Phillips-sama?"

I had heard that most students who choose the civil service track go on to Romano University.

"Yes, I want to become a diplomat."

Oh, I was just thinking he was like Percival when Russell chimed in.

"Phillips just wants to go around looking at foreign ruins. He’s a gutless coward who wants to be an archaeologist but got shot down by his parents."

Hmm, that sounds plausible too. Diplomats are civil servants, so wouldn't it be fine to visit ruins when they’re free?

"Russell, you’re the one who isn’t suited to be a diplomat. With that flippant attitude of yours, the Lawrence Kingdom would likely suffer."

Are both of you aspiring to be diplomats? Diplomats must be popular, after all.

"That’s not the case. More importantly, Patience, are you aiming for the diplomatic exam?"

I used to think that after graduating from the Royal Academy, I wanted to become a bureaucrat. But now, while I’ve decided to go to Romano University, the path beyond that is a maze.

"I don’t know yet. Besides, I’m not even sure if a woman can become a diplomat."

As we were talking, our Diplomacy teacher, Mr. Fochner, arrived in the classroom.

"A female diplomat! How wonderful. Part of a diplomat's job is to spread their own nation's culture. In this area, our country still lags behind the Sonia Kingdom, the Cordoba Kingdom, and the Estena Holy Empire. Patience, do your best."

Eh, I haven't even decided to become a diplomat yet. Still, is the Lawrence Kingdom considered culturally backward? Mr. Morse, the foreign language teacher, also said that Lawrence language is seen as stiff and lacking in artistry.

"Mr. Fochner, I cannot let that pass. The culture of the Lawrence Kingdom is incomparably superior to that of other countries."

Phillips is arguing back.

"I know that, but the reality is that we haven't been able to convey that to other nations. Romance culture doesn't flourish here as much as it does in the Sonia Kingdom, and we haven't adopted foreign culture as much as the Cordoba Kingdom. Besides, the Estena Holy Empire is obsessed solely with religion. The Lawrence Kingdom may sound good when called 'moderate,' but it lacks any distinct cultural features."

Wow, a debate looks like it’s about to explode. Mr. Fochner is good at riling people up.

"It must be admitted even by other nations that the Lawrence Kingdom’s culture is superior. Most magic tools are made in the Lawrence Kingdom, and most famous musical masterpieces are by our nation’s composers. The Sonia Kingdom only has romance novels, but ours also express the depths of the spiritual world."

Mr. Fochner shrugs at Russell's rebuttal.

"But other nations don't acknowledge them. The composers are indeed from our country, but they were active in the Estena Holy Empire. Furthermore, while magic tools were indeed invented in our country, they are most widely used in the Sonia Kingdom. And the Cordoba Kingdom controls the southern trade. Do you understand what the problem is?"

A debate erupted throughout the classroom. Since I’m in the Alchemy Club, I wondered why, despite magic tools being made in our country, they are used more in other nations.

"Is it perhaps because the Lawrence Kingdom's magic stones are expensive?"

In the impoverished Granger household, we couldn't buy magic stones, so we couldn't even use the toilet we had installed.

I hadn't even said it that loudly, but Mr. Fochner pointed right at me.

"Yes, Patience has noticed a key point. Our nation's magic stones are more expensive than those of other countries. Why do you think that is?"

Are there more monsters in other countries? Are they subjugating more? Or are they importing cheaper magic stones? The discussion grew heated.

"Well then, investigate this by next time! Not just about magic stones, but why are musicians active in the Estena Holy Empire? Why is our culture not evaluated by other nations? Choose any topic and summarize it in a report to submit."

Since World History is in the same classroom, we continue to talk during the break.

"What will you investigate, Lady Patience? Since you were the first to mention the price of magic stones, will you go with that?"

Phillips calling me "Lady" as always makes me feel a bit ticklish, but it's not so annoying that I want him to stop. It’s the feeling of "Miss" if "Sama" is "Lady," I suppose.

As for why musicians are active in the Estena Holy Empire, I sort of understood it from the lives of musicians in my previous life.

"Many people seem likely to write reports on magic stones. I thought it was because religious music is under the protection of the church, so there's no anxiety about one's livelihood. But I don't know if I can write a report on that."

Russell gave me advice, saying, "You should borrow and read 'Musician Berier' at the library."

"That book should have detailed information about his financial situation. I borrowed it because I like Berier's music, but I remember being quite disheartened by it. Well, you need money to live, but he probably never thought his diary would be published after his death."

Ah, that happens, doesn't it? If a great person's diary has detailed financial matters written in it, you'd be a little surprised. But I think it’s interesting because you can understand their life at the time. It seems my sensibilities are different from those of the nobility after all.

In World History, we covered the Kazaria Empire bringing the western half of the continent under its control. It ended just before the expedition to the east was about to begin. It’s just like the expedition of Alexander the Great from my previous life. This part is long and complicated. They go all over the place, you see.

Phillips seems to love Emperor Hadrianus, who led this expedition.

"I can't wait for the next class!" he said excitedly.

I’ve also grown accustomed to Russell looking exasperated.

I’m busy starting in the afternoon. In the third period, I have to go to the Alchemy Club and think about the structure of a bicycle. I plan to make the needle threader on Friday. It turns out that actually making things is best done on Fridays when I have time. The fourth period is Herbology. I’ve already passed Herbology, but I won’t let my guard down until I harvest the detox herbs. Besides, I have to plant the detox herbs in a different greenhouse.
