Spring has come!

Once March arrived, the cold and harsh winter softened just a little. I look forward to picking strawberries in the greenhouse with my younger siblings on the weekends.

As for my school life, I received my certificate of completion for art, and since I’ve been exempted from fourth-period classes except for dance on Tuesdays, things have become a bit easier. I use that time to prepare for math, language, history, classics, and magic studies.

I should be able to receive a certificate of completion in math by the final exams in June. For other subjects, when I become a third-year student next year, I feel I could skip a grade to the intermediate department on my own, without having to rely on Patience.

I have more breathing room in my studies now, but serving as a lady-in-waiting for Princess Margaret is as difficult as ever. In particular, preparing for the "Green Leaf Festival" in May takes up a lot of time. It’s painful that I’m losing the time I used to spend on side jobs to compose new songs for the music club's debut.

There is still a mountain of things to do to improve life for the Granger family. I am happy that the weather is warming up and firewood consumption is down, but I want to spend next winter even more comfortably.

In other words, I want to buy plenty of firewood during spring and summer, when demand is low. I want to stockpile it while it's cheap. Imagining the woodshed stacked high with firewood makes me impatient to earn more money. Patience died of pneumonia. It’s a matter of life and death.

"Patience, have you finished writing the sheet music for the new song?"

Rewriting piano pieces from my previous life into sheet music for the Hanon method takes time. I first have to practice them repeatedly using Hanon to correct for differences in the keyboard. Despite that, I’ve already submitted three songs, yet Princess Margaret still won't let me off the hook.

"The others have only submitted one, so wouldn't that be enough?"

I have grown used to being a lady-in-waiting, and when it’s just the two of us, I have started to offer a little pushback.

"Oh, no. We will be performing many times throughout the Green Leaf Festival. The more new songs we have, the better. Besides, you can't be playing the entire time, can you? You need to finish the sheet music quickly so the other members have time to practice."

That is no reason at all why I have to submit so many songs. I suspect this is nothing more than the selfishness of Princess Margaret, who loves music. I'll ask Club President Melissa about it at the next music club meeting. It seems best to avoid Vice President Albert. He’s an even bigger music lover than Princess Margaret.

Why is it that the people I want to avoid are the ones I end up meeting? When I arrived at the clubhouse with Princess Margaret and three school friends, Club President Melissa hadn't arrived yet, but Vice President Albert was waiting for us.

"Patience, is the sheet music for the new song finished?"

He ignores the aristocratic greetings due to Princess Margaret and thrusts his hand out toward me. He looks like a debt collector. How unlucky. Knock on wood, knock on wood!

"Albert, do try to settle down a bit."

Even Princess Margaret is exasperated. Catherine and the others are a bit angry too. But perhaps the nuance is a little different?

"Don't you think it's a bit much for only Lady Patience to be presenting new songs?"

That’s right, Catherine has a point.

"Princess Margaret, Lady Patience is getting tired, too."

You may sound spoiled, but Harriet's intentions are plain to see. I hope she does her best this time.

"What are you talking about, you fool? This is a club for competing in musical talent. Lady Catherine, Lady Harriet, Lady Liliana, why don't you all just submit several new pieces as well?"

Vice-President Albert shows no mercy even to young ladies of high nobility. Well, Albert is the second son of Duke Laforet, so it's no wonder he's fearless. I heard from Catherine or someone else that his grandfather is the late King's younger brother. Everyone is so knowledgeable about family lineage, but I don't have much interest in that. That sort of thing has nothing to do with me. Now, if we were talking about the price of firewood, that would be very interesting indeed.

The reason I'm escaping from reality like this is because I don't want to join the heated debate between Vice-President Albert and Catherine's group, so I'm sitting in a chair at the edge, staring blankly. I just want to hurry home to my room and do my side job.

"Patience, you look free. Play a new piece on the Hanon."

My attempt to sit there like a bystander was discovered. Told by Princess Margaret, I play a new piece on the Hanon. This time it isn't Mozart. It's a minuet by the great master Bach. It was really hard to put this into sheet music.

As expected, the music club members stop their heated debate once the performance begins. I'm glad it got quiet. The great master Bach should be listened to in silence.

Wait, it's too quiet. Oh no!

"Patience, will you marry me!"

Whoa, Vice-President Albert, please let go of my hand. I decline a marriage that’s just a substitute for a music player.

"Albert, do not take my attendant."

Whoa, has Princess Margaret decided that I'm her music player? I decline that too, but I am grateful that you made him let go of my hand.

"Catherine, Harriet, Liliana, you too should compose new pieces that aren't inferior to Patience's."

The three of them nodded in silence, but if they skip a grade to the middle school level next year, we'll be classmates. It would be bad if I didn't follow up on this somehow.

"Um, I have a request, if that's all right? I'm good at coming up with new phrases, but I'm not very good at polishing them into full pieces or transcribing them into sheet music."

Princess Margaret also knows that I have to replay and transcribe the music onto sheet music many times, so she understood what I was trying to say.

"That's right, you can just provide the phrases to everyone and have them compose the new pieces."

Vice-President Albert said that would be a waste of Patience's ideas, but Princess Margaret brushed him off, saying she wanted to hear many new pieces. Good grief.
